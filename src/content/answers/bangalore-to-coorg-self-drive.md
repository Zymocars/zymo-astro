    <p data-speakable="true" style="font-size:1.05rem;color:#FFFFFF;line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:1rem">Bangalore to Coorg (Madikeri) is about 255 km via NH275 and SH88, approximately 5-6 hours. The drive passes Mysore (a good pitstop at about 145 km), then enters the coffee and cardamom belt beyond Hunsur. The final 60 km from Hunsur to Madikeri has rolling hills, dense forest, and occasional morning fog. A hatchback handles the route in dry season; an SUV is more comfortable on the narrow estate roads inside Coorg beyond Madikeri.</p>
    
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Bangalore to Coorg route breakdown</h2><p>Bangalore → Mysore (NH275, about 145 km, 3 hrs): access-controlled expressway. Mysore → Hunsur (SH88, 50 km, 1 hr): transitions from Mysore plateau to the start of Western Ghats foothills. Hunsur → Madikeri (SH88 + local roads, 60 km, 1.5 hrs): this section gets rolling and narrow — good surface but requires care at blind corners. Madikeri to Virajpet (if heading south into Coorg): 30 km further, good road. Popular estates: Dubare Elephant Camp (44 km from Madikeri via Kushalnagar): add 1.5 hrs detour.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Best car and season for Bangalore-Coorg</h2><p>Car: a hatchback (Swift, i20, Baleno) handles Madikeri road in dry season. A compact SUV is better for estate-road exploration inside Coorg (many resorts have uneven approach roads). Season: October to March is optimal — dry weather, clear skies, coffee harvest (November-January) when the air carries the scent of ripe coffee. Monsoon (June-August): Coorg is lush green but roads can have fallen branches and fog — beautiful but drive carefully. April-May: pleasant with mild temperatures.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Cost breakdown for Bangalore-Coorg self-drive</h2><p>Car: hatchback ₹1,200-1,800/day; SUV ₹2,500-3,800/day. 2-day booking for Coorg: car ₹2,400-7,600. Fuel: about 510 km round trip; a hatchback at 16 km/L and ₹101/L = ₹3,220 round-trip. Tolls: Bengaluru-Mysuru Expressway about ₹370 one way for a car (₹185 on each of the expressway's two tolled sections, as of October 2026); other tolls vary. Resort room: ₹3,000-8,000/night (book early for weekends). Total for 2 people over 2 days with one resort night: about ₹9,360-21,022, before other tolls.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Fuel stops and road conditions by segment</h2><p>Mysore (about 145 km): stop at the Mysore Expressway exit — Shell, BPCL, and HP stations clustered here. Good point for a coffee break and to fuel for the next 100 km. Hunsur (190 km): fuel station before entering the coffee belt — last large town before Madikeri. Madikeri town: fuel stations available on the main road through town. Road conditions: Bangalore-Mysore NH275 is 6-lane smooth; Mysore-Hunsur SH88 is 2-lane good condition; Hunsur-Madikeri has intermittent narrow sections with plantation lorries — morning travel before 9 AM is easiest. Estate roads inside Coorg (to Dubare, Nagarhole, Iruppu Falls) are unmaintained tracks — compact SUV recommended for these detours.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Which car to book for Bangalore-Coorg on Zymo</h2><p>Swift or Baleno hatchback: perfectly capable for the Madikeri road in dry season — fuel-efficient and easy to park at the narrow coffee estate entrances. Hyundai Creta or Tata Nexon compact SUV: better if you plan to visit Nagarhole, Dubare Elephant Camp, or any plantation interior road. Innova Crysta: good for families of 6 on the main roads, but avoid estate detours in a long-wheelbase vehicle. For the most cost-effective 2-day Coorg trip, a hatchback from a Koramangala or Banashankari partner on Zymo is the smart choice — save SUV budget for routes that genuinely need it.</p></section>
