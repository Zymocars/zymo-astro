    <p data-speakable="true" style="font-size:1.05rem;color:#FFFFFF;line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:1rem">The best car for long drives on Indian roads combines three things: comfort over long distances (soft-tuned suspension, low cabin noise, adequate legroom), reliability with wide service availability (Maruti Suzuki, Hyundai, Tata, and Mahindra lead on this), and enough ground clearance for India's mix of highway, urban, and unmaintained rural sections. Compact SUVs like Hyundai Creta, Kia Seltos, Tata Nexon, and Maruti Grand Vitara currently lead most long-drive shortlists — they balance highway cruise comfort, road-presence, and city manoeuvrability better than either hatchbacks or full-size SUVs. For 4-5 travellers with luggage on a road trip, a compact SUV is the default. For 6-7 travellers, step up to Toyota Innova Crysta or Mahindra XUV700.</p>
    
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Criteria that actually matter on Indian long drives</h2><p>Ground clearance above 180mm — India's mix of highway, ghat, and unmaintained rural sections rewards clearance more than in most markets. Sedan owners regularly scrape underbody on rural stretches. Highway cruise comfort at 90-100 km/h — noise levels, seat contour, and rear-seat legroom matter more than 0-100 acceleration. Fuel economy above 15 km/l real-world — long-drive fuel bills add up fast at ₹105/litre; a 20 kmpl hatchback vs a 12 kmpl SUV differs by about ₹3,500 on a 1,000 km trip. Service network — Maruti, Hyundai, Tata, Mahindra have the widest coverage; a breakdown in Coorg or Uttarakhand with a niche brand can strand you. Boot space — 350+ litres for 4 travellers with luggage; 500+ litres for 5-6. Cabin insulation — ghat sections and highways over 4+ hours punish weak cabin insulation.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Best cars by trip type</h2><p>City-plus-occasional-road-trip (the most common Indian buying profile): compact SUVs — Hyundai Creta, Kia Seltos, Tata Nexon, Maruti Grand Vitara, Toyota Urban Cruiser Hyryder. Family road trip for 6-7 travellers: Toyota Innova Crysta and Innova Hycross remain the class benchmarks; Mahindra XUV700 is the modern seven-seater with premium interiors; Kia Carens covers 6-seater duty at a lower budget. Frequent hill and mountain drives: Mahindra Thar (2 or 4 door), Mahindra Scorpio N, Toyota Fortuner — all handle ghat elevation and rough sections that compact SUVs strain on. Cost-conscious long drives: Maruti Swift and Baleno hatchbacks deliver about 16-20 kmpl real-world and handle highway cruising well up to 4-5 travellers with modest luggage.</p></section>

Rear-seat legroom over 4 hours, cabin noise at 100 km/h on your route, whether the SUV fits your society parking, whether the boot swallows your family's weekend luggage — these emerge only in real use.
