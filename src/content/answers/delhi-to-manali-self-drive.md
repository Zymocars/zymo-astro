    <h1 style="color:#FFFFFF;font-size:1.6rem;margin-bottom:0.5rem">Delhi to Manali self-drive road trip — complete guide</h1>
    <p style="color:#8A94A6;font-size:0.85rem;margin-bottom:1.5rem">Updated June 2026 &middot; Based on partner policies across the Zymo network</p>
    
    
    
    <p data-speakable="true" style="font-size:1.05rem;color:#FFFFFF;line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:1rem">Delhi to Manali is about 530 km via NH44 and NH3, about 12-13 hours of driving with short stops. The route passes Chandigarh, Bilaspur, Mandi, and Kullu before entering Manali. An SUV (Creta, Scorpio N, XUV700) is strongly recommended — mandatory in monsoon and October-February when snow above 2,500m. Round-trip budget for 5 days: SUV booking ₹14,000-21,000, fuel about ₹9,250, plus tolls. Book on Zymo with Delhi partners in Gurgaon, Noida, or South Delhi.</p>
    
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Delhi to Manali route and key milestones</h2><p>NH44 Delhi to Chandigarh (260 km, 4-5 hours via Panipat, Ambala): six-lane highway, fastest section of the trip. Chandigarh to Bilaspur via NH205 (100 km, 2 hours): good road, first hill views appear. Bilaspur to Mandi via NH154 (65 km, 1.5 hours): begins the proper mountain driving with switchbacks after Sundernagar. Mandi to Kullu via NH3 (55 km, 1.5 hours): valley driving along Beas river, good tarmac. Kullu to Manali via NH3 (40 km, 1 hour): narrows through Dobhi and Bhuntar, can be slow with tourist traffic in peak season. Atal Tunnel (Rohtang Bypass, 9 km): takes you to Sissu valley — no Rohtang permit needed for the tunnel. Rohtang Pass (alternative to Atal Tunnel): open roughly May-October, with a permit required online from the Himachal Pradesh government's Rohtang permit portal.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Cost breakdown for Delhi-Manali self-drive</h2><p>Car: an SUV (Creta, Scorpio N, XUV700) at ₹2,800-4,200 per day × 5 days = ₹14,000-21,000. Fuel: the round trip is about 1,060 km; at 11 km/L and ₹96/L that is about ₹9,250. Tolls: tolls vary by route and vehicle class and are paid via FASTag — check current rates in your FASTag or NHAI app before you leave. Parking in Manali: ₹50-100 per day at most guesthouses. Total non-refundable cost of car and fuel for 2-3 people sharing: about ₹23,250-30,250 for 5 days, plus tolls and parking — cheaper than an equivalent holiday package.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Season guide and what to prepare for</h2><p>June to September (monsoon season): road open to Manali, green valleys, frequent rain above Kullu — landslide risk on NH3 between Mandi and Kullu; check HP PWD road alerts before departure. October to November: best weather for Manali driving — clear skies, light snow on peaks, moderate crowds. December to March: Rohtang Pass closed with heavy snow; Atal Tunnel stays open till extreme snow. Drive into Manali is possible December-February but chain-assist may be required above Kullu — confirm rental partner allows winter mountain use. April to May: melt season, mud patches on NH3 above Mandi; Rohtang usually reopens in May. What to carry: snow chains (December-February — confirm with partner if fitted), offline navigation (Maps.me or Google Maps offline area download for Bilaspur-Manali), warm layers even for June-September (Manali nights drop to 10-12°C), emergency contact card with the partner's roadside-assistance number.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Fuel stops and road conditions by segment</h2><p>Panipat (95 km from Delhi, NH44): large petrol stations on both sides of highway — fill up here. Ambala bypass (220 km): Chandigarh fuel 40 km ahead; some drivers top up at Ambala for peace of mind. Chandigarh Sector 43 petrol station (260 km): the last fuel stop before serious mountain driving — fill full tank. Mandi city (420 km): essential fuel and rest stop before the Kullu-Manali stretch. Road conditions: Delhi-Chandigarh NH44 is excellent 6-lane; Chandigarh-Mandi is good 2-lane highway with occasional potholes near Sundernagar; Mandi-Kullu-Manali NH3 has better surface quality than most hill roads but narrows in Dobhi and Bhuntar.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Which car to book for Delhi-Manali on Zymo</h2><p>Hyundai Creta or Kia Seltos: ideal for 3-4 passengers — good ground clearance, capable on mountain roads, fuel-efficient diesel variants available. Mahindra Scorpio N or XUV700: best for 5-6 passengers with luggage — extra torque on the Mandi-Manali climbs, roof-mounted luggage carrier if needed. Tata Safari: comfortable alternative for a family of 5. Hatchbacks (Swift, i20) are manageable in June-October on the main highway but are undersized for Rohtang or Chandratal side-trips — rent an SUV and don't compromise on this route. Book from Gurgaon or Noida partners for best Delhi-departure inventory.</p></section>
    
    
    
    <section id="faq">
    <h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Related questions</h2>
    <dl>
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">Is a self-drive car allowed from Delhi to Manali?</dt>
    <dd>Yes. Delhi to Manali is a standard outstation self-drive route. Confirm at booking that the partner allows Himachal Pradesh outstation and declare Manali as the destination at partner handover.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">What is the best car for Delhi to Manali self-drive?</dt>
    <dd>An SUV is strongly recommended — Hyundai Creta, Kia Seltos, Mahindra Scorpio N, Mahindra XUV700. The NH3 above Mandi has tight switchbacks and occasional loose surface. In monsoon (July-September) and winter (October-February), ground clearance and traction control matter. Hatchbacks and sedans should be avoided on this route.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">How long does Delhi to Manali take by road?</dt>
    <dd>About 12-13 hours of driving with short stops. Delhi to Chandigarh is about 260 km (4-5 hours); Chandigarh to Manali is about 270 km (6-7 hours with mountain driving). Most travellers stop overnight in Chandigarh or Mandi and complete the trip the next morning.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">What is the Rohtang Pass self-drive permit requirement?</dt>
    <dd>Rohtang Pass requires an online permit from the Himachal Pradesh government's Rohtang permit portal — apply in advance. Permits are issued per vehicle per day, for a fee. The Atal Tunnel (Rohtang Bypass) does not require a Rohtang permit and stays open through most of the year, closing when heavy snow blocks the approach road.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">How much does it cost to self-drive Delhi to Manali?</dt>
    <dd>Round-trip budget for 5 days: SUV booking ₹14,000-21,000 (₹2,800-4,200/day × 5 days), fuel about ₹9,250 (about 1,060 km round-trip), tolls (paid via FASTag), and the Rohtang permit fee if applicable. Total: about ₹23,250-30,250 plus tolls and the permit for 2-3 people sharing — meaningfully cheaper than an equivalent holiday package with driver.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">What is the best time of year for a Delhi to Manali self-drive?</dt>
    <dd>May-June and September-October are optimal. May-June: roads clear, Rohtang open, peak season. September-October: post-monsoon visibility, fewer crowds. Avoid July-August (heavy landslide risk on NH3 above Mandi). December-February: Manali town is reachable but roads above Atal Tunnel close unpredictably.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">Can I take a self-drive car from Manali to Spiti Valley?</dt>
    <dd>The Spiti route (Kunzum La, 4,551m) requires prior partner approval and a high-clearance vehicle with appropriate insurance. Most standard partner cars on Zymo are not cleared for this terrain. Confirm explicitly with the partner before booking if Spiti or Lahaul beyond Atal Tunnel is your destination.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">Do I need snow tyres for Delhi to Manali in winter?</dt>
    <dd>For December-February travel above Kullu, chains or snow tyres may be required at checkposts. Standard partner cars on Zymo are not equipped for January-February snowfall above 2,500m. October and late March are safer windows for cold-weather driving without the snow tyre requirement.</dd>
    </dl>
