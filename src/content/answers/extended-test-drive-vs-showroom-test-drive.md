    <h1 style="color:#FFFFFF;font-size:1.6rem;margin-bottom:0.5rem">Extended Test Drive vs the short test drive: what's the difference?</h1>
    <p style="color:#8A94A6;font-size:0.85rem;margin-bottom:1.5rem">Updated June 2026 &middot; Across the Zymo network</p>
    
    <p data-speakable="true" style="font-size:1.05rem;color:#FFFFFF;line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:1rem">The short test drive is a salesperson-accompanied drive on a route the dealer chose. It shows the car exists. An Extended Test Drive is a one-day-or-longer self-drive booking on your own routes, your own parking, and your own weekend. It shows whether the car fits your life. Both serve different stages of the buying decision — do both, in that order.</p>

    <section>
    <h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Why a short test drive is structurally limited</h2>
    <p>Showroom test drives demonstrate the car's strengths in a controlled environment. The route is pre-selected for smooth roads and light traffic. The car is pre-conditioned (cleaned, fuelled, warmed up). The salesperson steers attention to features that close sales. None of this is wrong: it is what a short test drive is for. It is just not what the buying decision needs.</p>
    </section>
    
    <section>
    <h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Use both in this order</h2>
    <ol>
    <li><strong>Showroom visit first.</strong> Take the short test drive. Negotiate price. Understand variants. Get the dealer's quote in writing.</li>
    <li><strong>Then book the Extended Test Drive on Zymo.</strong> Choose the shortlisted model (or two — same routes, different weekends). Drive it through your life.</li>
    <li><strong>Return to the dealer.</strong> Close the deal with confidence — or walk away with the right reason. Either outcome is better than the alternative of regret after signing.</li>
    </ol>
    </section>
    
    <section>
    <h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Does it harm the dealer relationship?</h2>
    <p>No. You still purchase the car through the dealer. The Extended Test Drive is a decision tool, not a transaction channel. You go back to the dealer knowing whether the car fits your life.</p>
    </section>
    
    <section id="faq">
    <h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Related questions</h2>
    <dl>
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">What does the short test drive cover?</dt>
    <dd>A salesperson-accompanied drive on a pre-selected route, typically a smooth arterial road in light traffic at a time of day chosen for the car's best performance. It shows you the car exists, the seat is comfortable for a short loop, and the infotainment screen turns on. It does not show you the buying-decision details: your real commute, your parking spot, your family seating reality, or the car's behaviour at month-three boredom.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">Why is a short test drive not enough?</dt>
    <dd>Most car-purchase regrets surface only after sustained use: boot space inadequacy on a family trip, rear-seat discomfort on a long drive, suspension behaviour over your local potholes, the position of the gear lever when you reverse-park into your specific society. A short test drive on a chosen route filters out the exact information that decides whether you should buy the car.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">How does an Extended Test Drive solve this?</dt>
    <dd>By giving you the car on your routes, in your traffic, at your parking, for your weekend trip. You experience the car the way you will own it, not the way it gets shown at the showroom. The decision you make at the end is based on lived experience, not on a short test drive impression.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">Should I do both?</dt>
    <dd>Yes — in that order. Visit the showroom first for the short test drive and to negotiate price. Then book an Extended Test Drive for the shortlisted model (or two) on Zymo. Then return to the dealer to close, knowing how the car fits your routes, parking, and family.</dd>
    
    <dt style="color:#FFFFFF;font-weight:600;margin-top:1rem">Does the dealer get the credit for the sale if I do an Extended Test Drive on Zymo first?</dt>
    <dd>Yes. You still purchase the car through the dealer. The Extended Test Drive is a decision tool, not a transaction channel. The dealer relationship for the actual purchase remains unchanged.</dd>
    </dl>
