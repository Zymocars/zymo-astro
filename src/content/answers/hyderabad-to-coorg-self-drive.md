    <p data-speakable="true" style="font-size:1.05rem;color:#FFFFFF;line-height:1.8;margin-bottom:1rem">Hyderabad to Coorg (Madikeri) is about 825 km via NH44 towards Bangalore, then NH275 and SH88 to Madikeri — approximately 14-15 hours of driving. The route passes through Bangalore (a natural overnight stop at the midpoint). An SUV is comfortable; a hatchback handles the final Hunsur-Madikeri ghat stretch in dry season. Round-trip budget: car ₹8,800-12,000 for 4 days, fuel about ₹15,750, plus tolls.</p>
    
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Route breakdown and key milestones</h2><p>Hyderabad → Bangalore via NH44 (570 km, 9-10 hrs): a 4-6 lane highway via Kurnool and Anantapur. Most drivers stop overnight at Bangalore. Bangalore → Mysore via NH275 (about 145 km, 3 hrs): access-controlled expressway. Mysore → Hunsur → Madikeri via SH88 (110 km, 2.5 hrs): rolling hill road through coffee estates. Total one-way: about 825 km. Day 1: Hyderabad-Bangalore. Day 2: Bangalore-Coorg (morning drive). Day 3-4: Coorg exploration. Day 5: Coorg-Bangalore-Hyderabad (long day) or split over 2 days.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Cost breakdown for Hyderabad-Coorg self-drive</h2><p>Car: compact SUV ₹2,200-3,000/day × 4 days = ₹8,800-12,000. Fuel: approximately 1,650 km round trip; petrol SUV at 11 km/L at ₹105/L = about ₹15,750. Tolls: Bengaluru-Mysuru Expressway about ₹370 one way for a car (₹185 on each of the expressway's two tolled sections, as of October 2026); other tolls vary by route and vehicle class and are paid via FASTag — check current rates in your FASTag or NHAI app before you leave. Car and fuel for 4 days: approximately ₹24,500-28,000, plus tolls and your stay.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Season guide for Hyderabad-Coorg</h2><p>October to March: optimal — dry weather, coffee harvest November-January, pleasant Coorg temperatures. June-August monsoon: Coorg is lush green but NH44 through Anantapur can have waterlogged sections during heavy rain; Madikeri roads have fallen branches in monsoon. April-May: hot through the Deccan plateau but cool in Coorg — manageable. The Hunsur-Madikeri section has intermittent fog December-February mornings — drive slowly if you arrive in early morning.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Fuel stops and road conditions by segment</h2><p>Kurnool (220 km from Hyderabad, NH44): large fuel cluster on NH44 — first major pit stop, good for a break. Anantapur (350 km): another large NH44 town with multiple fuel stations. Bangalore outskirts (NICE Road interchange, 570 km): top up before the Mysore Expressway. Hunsur (about 765 km from Hyderabad, 60 km before Madikeri): last fuel before Coorg — essential stop. Road conditions: NH44 Hyderabad-Bangalore is 4-6 lane with patches being upgraded near Kurnool; Mysore Expressway is excellent 6-lane; Hunsur-Madikeri SH88 is 2-lane with plantation lorries.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Which car to book for Hyderabad-Coorg on Zymo</h2><p>Hyundai Creta or Kia Seltos: ideal — comfortable for the 9-hour NH44 run and capable on the Madikeri ghat road. Innova Crysta: best for 5-6 people with heavy luggage over 4 days — boot space and rear comfort justify the cost on a long trip. Swift or hatchback: manages the route in dry season but rear-passenger comfort on NH44's 9 hours is tighter. Book from Banjara Hills or Gachibowli partners on Zymo for Hyderabad-side pickup.</p></section>
