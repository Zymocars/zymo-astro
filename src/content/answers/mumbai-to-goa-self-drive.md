The route passes Panvel, Pune bypass, Kolhapur, Sawantwadi, and the Goa border at Patradevi. Best car: compact SUV (Creta, Nexon, Brezza) for the Sahyadri ghat stretches.     <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Route options and key milestones</h2><p>NH66 coastal route (590 km, 10-11 hours) is the standard Mumbai-Goa road and has good tarmac most of the year. Milestones: Panvel toll (Mumbai bypass ₹160), Pune bypass via NH48, Kolhapur (450 km from Mumbai — good overnight stop), Sawantwadi, Patradevi Goa border. NH48 inland route via Pune adds 20-30 km but avoids some Konkan coastal narrows. Drive recommendation: start before 6 AM from Mumbai to clear the city and Expressway before morning peak; reach Kolhapur by noon for a break; Goa by evening. Monsoon (July-August) warning: NH66 through Konkan has landslide risk near Ratnagiri and Khed — check MoRTH or Google Maps alerts before the trip.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Mumbai to Goa trip cost breakdown</h2><p>Car booking: a compact SUV (Creta, Nexon, Brezza) for 3 days on Zymo runs ₹6,600-9,000 (₹2,200-3,000 per day). Fuel: round trip is approximately 1,180 km; at 14 km/L in a petrol SUV at ₹105/L, that is ₹8,850 — budget ₹9,000. Tolls: NH66 Mumbai to Goa one-way is approximately ₹640 via FASTag (Mumbai-Pune Expressway ₹320, Khed ₹85, Kolhapur ₹120, Goa border ₹115). Round trip: ₹1,280. Security deposit (refundable): ₹8,000-12,000 held on your card. Total non-refundable trip cost for 2 people: approximately ₹16,000-20,000 vs intercity cab for the same route (₹25,000-35,000 round-trip).</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Best season and what to carry</h2><p>Best time to drive Mumbai to Goa: October to February. Roads are clear, Goa weather is mild, beaches are full. March to May is passable but hot — the ghats and Konkan strip get to 38-40°C. June to September monsoon: NH66 has landslide debris risk near Ratnagiri and Amboli ghat — avoid unless you have an SUV and check real-time alerts. What to carry: driving licence originals, partner rental agreement (partner hands this over at pickup), Zymo booking confirmation on phone, FASTag works on the car (fitted by partner), first-aid kit. Google Maps works all the way to Patradevi Goa border; Airtel and Jio coverage holds on NH66 with minor dead spots near Phonda tunnel.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Fuel stops and road conditions by segment</h2><p>Panvel toll exit (50 km): last Mumbai suburb fuel station — fill up fully here before the Expressway. Khopoli (80 km, base of Khandala ghat): fuel stations on service road, good pit stop. Kolhapur (450 km): midpoint — large city with multiple fuel stations on NH66, recommended lunch stop. Sawantwadi (540 km): last major fuel stop before the Goa border — fill up here. Road conditions: NH66 from Panvel to Goa is 4-lane divided highway for most of the route; 2-lane with passing bays near Ratnagiri and Khed — stay in lane, no overtaking on blind curves. The Shirdi-Pune bypass (if using NH48 inland variant) has excellent surface all the way.</p></section>
    <section><h2 style="color:#FFFFFF;margin:2rem 0 0.5rem;font-size:1.4rem">Which car to book for this route on Zymo</h2><p>Compact SUV (Hyundai Creta, Tata Nexon, Maruti Brezza): best for Mumbai-Goa in all seasons — ground clearance handles the Konkan coastal undulations and monsoon road debris. Higher seating gives better visibility on the mountain stretches. Toyota Innova Crysta: best for families of 5-6 with luggage — boot space for 4 large bags and comfortable rear seat. Hatchback (Swift, i20): acceptable in October-May dry weather on NH66; avoid in monsoon as the ghat section requires more torque and clearance than entry-level hatchbacks offer. Book on Zymo with Andheri, Powai, or Thane partners for the best Mumbai-departure inventory.</p></section>
